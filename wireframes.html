rhrs design

Three things the app cannot do yet.

Who is on a script and what they may touch, who plays which part and in what voice, and how a script gets in at all. Grey on purpose: these are the shapes and the words, not the design. The copy is real — at this stage the words are the part worth arguing about. From the meeting of 21 September 2026.

a screen a sheet, over whatever you are on text that is not the point here what takes you on tap a name to copy it

Every box is named, and the name in its header is the one to use everywhere — here, in conversation, and in the prototype's own list of screens. Where the screen is already built, the name is the one the prototype's panel gives it, letter for letter, so there is never a second word for the same thing. Tap a name to copy it.

The prototype is drawn for whoever is looking. Its panel has a You are switch — Luky (admin, cast as Nina), Luděk and Petr (readers, both cast as Michael), Jana (editor, not cast), Tomas (reader, not cast) — and every screen subtracts what that member may not do, from the permission table below. Controls disappear, facts stay, and where silence would confuse a sentence says who does it. The boxes marked as a member sees it are the record of those subtractions, so they can be argued with here.

One

Members and roles

Membership belongs to a script, not to a company: a role is held on one script and an invitation is an invitation into one script. Everyone reads and listens to everything; what differs is what they may change.

Two questions, not one. What is this person allowed to do to the script — reader, editor, admin — and, separately, are they cast in a part. They are independent: an admin can act, an actor can be nothing more than a reader who holds a part, and a director who never goes on stage is an editor with no part at all. Rolling the two into one list of four would say the opposite.

English does us a favour: the past of to cast is cast, so the word works as the fact, the act and the state at once — Cast in a part on the button, Cast as Michael against the name, Not cast when there is nothing. No second word for it anywhere. Holding a part adds, whatever the role:

Which turns the old rule around, and better: it is not that a reader has no Rehearse — it is that nobody who is not cast has one. An admin who is not in the play has nothing to rehearse either, and now the app says so without a special case.

Two

Cast, alternations and voices

One actor can hold several parts and one part can be held by several actors — the alternation a theatre runs on. Everything else follows from that: whose take plays when two people have recorded the same line, and what a part sounds like before anybody has. The voice is the script's, assigned by the admin; which alternate you hear is yours.

Who you hear — one screen, four ways in

Choosing an alternate is not editing the script: it is how this production sounds to you, and everyone in the cast may do it. So it is its own screen, listing every part that has more than one actor, and the three places it is reached from are the three places the question comes up — while you listen, while you look at the script, and while you are standing on the part itself.

The voice, where the audio is

A take belongs to the actor who recorded it; the voice belongs to the part. Audio edit shows your takes and nobody else's, so a second actor in the part never appears in it — what they change is what a listener hears, and the listener chooses between actors in Who you hear, never between takes. Two things follow. The generated voice can be named wherever it is played, because there is one per part however many actors hold it. And the place to say what you will actually hear on a line is the line's own menu — under the three dots, where a question about one line is asked — not every card in Listen.

A script gets in by being read by the machine and checked by a person, in that order and in three passes: the characters it found, the scenes it split, and finally the lines. Settled, and still open

The decisions behind these boxes

Four of the five questions these wireframes were drawn around were answered on 22 September. Casting is the admin's alone — nobody claims a free part. A reader loses the Recording wizard and the missing-audio notification along with Rehearse, because none of them have anything to say to someone who does not record. A private note can hang on anybody's line — it is yours, nobody else sees it, and the line it is about is often not your own. A standalone direction gets a reference of its own, #001-D02, the way a line has #001-0002: it is quoted in messages just as often.

And the fifth: an import left half-way keeps a draft. It sits on All scripts as Draft · import not checked and carries on where it was left. Nothing in these three blocks is waiting on a decision any more.
