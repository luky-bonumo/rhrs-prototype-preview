Screen by screen

What each one is for

The same list as the map, in reading order, with a note on what the screen has to do.

Login Apple, Google, or email and then a six-digit code. The two providers are shown identically — Apple asks to be no less prominent than any other, and equal treatment also stops the screen naming a right answer; through either of them there is no code and no name step, because the name arrives with the account. The email route is one route rather than two — there is nothing to choose between signing in and signing up, so a new account is one extra screen (your name) instead of a separate path. In the prototype the code 000000 is wrong and 111111 has expired; nina@example.com is an existing account and anything else is new. Drawn
Dashboard Landing screen. Where you left off, last opened scripts, shortcuts, and the week's stats. The only screen on a wine background. Drawn
Notifications A sheet from the bell on the dashboard. Two kinds: lines of yours still without audio, naming the scene and the script and leading into the wizard, and a note that shaking the phone sends feedback. The bell's dot is whether anything is unread; they are marked read when the sheet closes, so you can still see which were new while it is open. Sheet
Global settings Account and Appearance are drawn — the way into your account, and the light / dark switch so the prototype can be looked at on a phone in either mode. Voices, rehearsal defaults, notifications, storage and subscription are still to come, and the screen says so. Part drawn
Account You, rather than a character: the name the rest of the cast sees against your lines, edited in the same field as everything else that is written in the app, the address your sign-in code goes to, and the way out. From Global settings or from the account button on any mode's settings sheet. Drawn
All scripts Every script, with scene counts and whether you have a part in it. Drawn
Script detail Where you left off in coral, Whole script in wine, the Recording wizard's card with how many of your lines still have no audio, the Cast card, then the scenes. An admin has the pencil in the corner; a member has the alternation glyph there instead, and it opens Who you hear. Drawn
Read Plain reading. Lines separated by rules, private notes and directions, no playback. Drawn
Listen The scene plays itself. Lines become cards, the one being read is outlined, and the transport takes the colour of whoever is speaking. Drawn
Rehearse Cue and answer. Someone else's line plays as audio in wine; your own line records in coral with the text hidden. Drawn
Evaluation What the app heard you say, against the line as written. Opens from Rehearse and from either recorder, and closes back to whichever it was. When the take itself is the problem — quiet, muffled — a block under the transcript says so and what to do about it, because the words above it may not be the ones you said. Drawn
Line menu Per-line actions: start here in Listen or Rehearse, edit the line, add a private note, and — on a line of yours — its audio. Sheet
Mode settings Settings for the mode you are in — text size, which lines show, note defaults, playback speed, what the narrator reads, and in Listen and Rehearse the way into Who you hear. Sheet
Playback speed Two speeds, yours and theirs, as presets over a stepper. From the figure under the scrubber or from Mode settings; in Rehearse only theirs, because you say your own. Sheet
Note editor Writing a private note straight from the line menu, without going through Line edit. A line's own text, its note and its direction are already editable in Line edit, and a standalone direction has a screen of its own; this is the shortcut, and it is not drawn yet. To design
Wizard — scenes Step one of recording: which scene. Every scene of the script with what it is still short, a scene you are not in shown but inert, and a finished one marked as recorded rather than hidden. Drawn
Recording Wizard Rehearse narrowed to one job. The list is the whole scene — cue lines, directions and private notes included — and the filter (Unrecorded / All my lines) decides where the wizard takes you, not what you can see. The recorder is the one from Rehearse: left to run it keeps the take, stopped by hand it goes to Evaluation, where Use keeps it and Retake throws it away. A line that already has audio opens its takes in Audio edit. Drawn
Wizard settings Auto-advance, play the cue first, and step through every line the way Rehearse does. Sheet
Edit script People and Cast at the top, each with its count, then the real list of scenes — reordered by the handle on the right, opened into their lines. Delete script at the bottom, which asks. Drawn
People Who is on this script, from Edit script. A role — reader, editor, admin — and, separately, whether they hold a part: the two are independent, so an admin can act and an actor can be nothing more than a reader who holds a part. Drawn
Person One person: the parts they hold first, the role second. Removing them asks, and says what does not happen — their takes stay with the script and the part comes free. Drawn
Invite A link that carries the role and, when the admin already knows it, the part. Choosing the part swaps the sheet rather than stacking one on another. Sheet
Join What somebody sees arriving from an invitation link: who invited them, the script, the role, and the part if the admin already set one. Nobody claims a free part — the director casts. Drawn
Cast The parts and who holds them, with the narrator's voice at the top. A part can have more than one actor and a person more than one part. An admin opens a part from here; a member sees the same list read-only and, on an alternated part, which of the actors they hear. Drawn
Edit character The name, the cast with how much of the part each actor has recorded, You hear when the part has two actors, and the voice heard until a line is recorded. Drawn
Voice picker The voices for a part or for the narrator, each with a preview that reads this part's first line — the question is never how a voice sounds but how it sounds saying this. Admin only. A Suggested group sits on top once there is one. Sheet
Who you hear Every part with more than one actor, and which of them plays for you. Yours alone — nobody else is affected. From Listen and Rehearse settings, from the part, from a member's Cast, and from the corner of a member's Script detail. Drawn
Scene edit The items of one scene, lines and standalone directions alike, dragged by the handle on the right; a small (+) between two rows adds a line or a direction right there. Drawn
Line edit One line: its character, text, private note, direction and audio — every field the same editable component. Reached from a line's menu, from Scene edit and from the wizard. Drawn
Audio edit The takes of one of your lines, newest first. In use picks the one everybody hears; the generated voice sits underneath as the default, so the line is never silent. Drawn
Direction edit A standalone direction, with a reference of its own. Its menu in Read leads here too. Drawn
Add here A line or a direction, before the row you tapped between. Sheet
Alerts One sheet, one table of words: delete a recording, a take, a line, a direction or a script; take a part away; remove somebody from the script; sign out; record all your lines; and Android's playback controls after notifications were refused. Sheet
New script Three ways in: a file, pasted text, or nothing at all. From the plus on All scripts and the row under All scripts on the dashboard — for whoever runs a script somewhere. Start from nothing makes an untitled script with one scene and opens Edit script on its name. Drawn
Choose a file One big target that stands for the system's picker, and the formats it takes. The prototype picks a file for you. Drawn
Paste the text A field for the whole script and a note on how names and directions are read; joins the same road at Kind of script. Drawn
Kind of script Theatre or screen, and what to bring in as well — staging directions, or acting and filming directions apart. What is switched off is not imported at all. Drawn
Reading the script Four steps and a bar. The script exists from here on: leaving keeps a draft on All scripts that opens where you left it. Drawn
Could not read it Two failures, two messages — no text in the file, or text with nobody speaking in it — each offering the way out that would work. Nothing was read, so there is no draft. Drawn
Imported characters The first pass: the names the file gave, each with its line count. A row opens Edit character to rename it, or to delete a heading taken for a name. Drawn
Imported scenes The second pass: the scenes it split, with the first direction as a hint. A row opens Scene edit on the scene's name. Drawn
Imported script The third pass: scene by scene into the lines. Finish ends the wizard — confirming is not saving, nothing is written twice. Drawn
Imported scene Scene edit while a script is being checked: the name, the rows into Line edit and Direction edit, no adding and no dragging, and Something is wrong here at the bottom. A state of Scene edit, not a screen. State
Request edit The same small loop in every pass: what is wrong, where to find it in the file, and — in the lines pass only — whether it applies to the whole script. Sheet
Request working A bar and a promise of how long. The prototype answers by hand: the first request in a pass comes back fixed, every one after it does not. Drawn
Request fixed What changed, and the way back to the list. In the characters pass the fix is real — the heading taken for a name is gone. Drawn
Request failed By hand, here or on the web — and, said out loud, that everything else is kept. Drawn
Import finished The end of one job is the start of the next: the counts, then Cast the parts and Invite the others, then the script itself. Drawn
Self tape Recording an audition to camera. Shortcut exists on the dashboard, screen not yet drawn. To design
My characters The roles you play across all scripts. Shortcut exists on the dashboard, screen not yet drawn. To design
